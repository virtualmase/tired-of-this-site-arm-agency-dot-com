Self-guided orientation

Is this a buyer-confidence problem—or just a visibility question?

Use this short orientation to decide whether the conditions exist for a meaningful AI Buyer Intelligence Sprint. This page does not score your company and does not replace a human fit assessment.

Five conditions to check

The Sprint is useful only when a buyer decision is both expensive and actionable.

Answer these questions honestly. If most answers are yes, a Category Presence Brief is the appropriate next step.

01

Is there a commercial event inside the next 180 days?

A product or category launch, enterprise move, pipeline issue, competitive shift, transaction, or public-trust event gives the work a real decision date.

02

Does the relevant sale justify the effort?

Typical fit is a $75,000+ annual contract value, $250,000+ lifetime value, or a strategic decision with material downside.

03

Can you name the buyer question that matters?

For example: “Are we credible at enterprise scale?” “How do we compare on implementation?” or “Can a regulated customer trust the evidence?”

04

Do you have buyer voice or pipeline evidence?

Sales-call themes, win/loss data, RFP questions, support issues, product documentation, and analytics are enough. ARM does not need unrestricted system access.

05

Can someone act on a finding in 90 days?

At minimum, a sponsor needs product marketing and sales involvement, plus the relevant web, product, security, services, legal, or customer-proof owners.

If these conditions are present, do not start with a score.

Start with the category, buyer, trigger, and alternatives. ARM will determine whether a fixed-scope, 15-business-day Sprint is warranted and what evidence is needed to make it useful.
