Living vocabulary · version 1.0

The AI Buyer Intelligence glossary.

Plain-language definitions for enterprise buying groups, intent and signal systems, agentic execution, and the evidence-led artifacts ARM uses. Terms describe an operating model—not guaranteed platform behavior or an external standard.

42 TERMS
Definition boundary. “ARM uses” identifies ARM Agency’s working definition. Other terms are common industry language with usage that may vary by company, vendor, or research context.

Buyer and revenue systems

Commercial context

AI Buyer Intelligence

ARM’s evidence-led practice of mapping the questions, sources, proof, and conversion conditions that shape a high-value B2B buyer decision.

High-ACV B2B

Business-to-business sales with high annual contract value or comparable strategic significance, typically involving extended evaluation and multiple stakeholders.

Commercial trigger

An event that gives buyer-intelligence work a decision date, such as a category move, launch, pipeline problem, competitive shift, transaction, or trust event.

Category creation

The work of defining and establishing a new market frame, buyer problem, comparison set, and language for an emerging type of solution.

Category redefinition

A deliberate change to how an existing market, problem, or alternative set is framed for buyers.

Product or feature launch

A commercial event that introduces a new offer or capability and creates fresh questions about fit, implementation, proof, and adoption.

Pipeline acceleration

Work intended to resolve specific evidence, stakeholder, or decision friction in active opportunities; it does not mean manufacturing urgency or guaranteeing velocity.

Competitive conquesting

A focused effort to win accounts considering a named competitor or status-quo alternative by addressing decision-relevant differences with supported evidence.

People and buying groups

Decision structure

Buying committee

The group of stakeholders who influence, approve, evaluate, implement, use, or block a B2B purchase.

Buying group

The in-scope subset of people and roles involved in a particular decision. ARM does not assume a fixed committee size.

Champion

An internal stakeholder who actively supports the change and helps the buying group navigate evidence, consensus, and process.

Economic buyer

The person or governing group with authority over the budget and business outcome attached to a purchase.

Blocker

A stakeholder, requirement, or unresolved condition capable of delaying or preventing a decision.

Multithreading

Building relevant, role-aware relationships across multiple members of a buying group instead of relying on one contact.

Single-threaded deal risk

The exposure created when an opportunity depends on one relationship and lacks access to other decision makers, evaluators, or blockers.

Identity resolution

The process of connecting signals or records to the correct person, account, role, or organization while preserving uncertainty and privacy boundaries.

Signals and orchestration

Evidence inputs

Buyer intent

Observed behavior that may indicate research or interest. Intent is a probabilistic signal, not proof that a person will buy.

Dark funnel

Buyer research and influence that occurs outside the seller’s directly attributable channels, including private conversations, communities, and unobserved content consumption.

Conversation intelligence

Structured analysis of approved call, meeting, or message data to identify questions, objections, commitments, and recurring themes.

Technographics

Information about the technologies an organization uses or may use. Coverage, freshness, and inference quality vary by source.

Product usage signal

An approved observation about product activity that may inform adoption, expansion, support, or risk analysis when interpreted in context.

Signal orchestration

The governed process of combining relevant signals, sources, conditions, and ownership into a usable decision workflow.

Account health score

A composite indicator derived from selected account signals. It is a decision aid whose inputs and limits should remain inspectable—not an objective statement of account truth.

High-intent threshold

A documented rule for when selected intent evidence warrants review or action. Crossing a threshold does not prove purchase intent.

Agentic execution

Governed action

Agentic execution

Software-led completion of bounded tasks under an explicit mandate, with defined inputs, permissions, stop conditions, and human accountability.

Agent-assisted content generation

Using AI to draft or transform approved source material into a defined content artifact subject to review and publication authority.

Closed-loop automation

A workflow that observes a defined signal, applies a rule, initiates an approved action, and records the result for review.

Mandate

The documented principal, objective, permission, resource, time, escalation, and revocation boundaries governing an agent or automated workflow.

Human-in-the-loop

A control pattern requiring a named person to review, approve, intervene in, or accept selected system actions.

Acceptance test

An observable condition used to decide whether a deliverable or action meets the agreed requirement.

ARM method and artifacts

Current offer

Category Presence Brief

ARM’s no-cost, human-reviewed fit assessment based on the category, buyer, trigger, alternatives, timing, and available evidence. It is not a public score or free audit.

AI Buyer Intelligence Sprint

ARM’s $12,500 fixed-scope, 15-business-day engagement producing four decision artifacts after fit and written scope acceptance.

Buyer conversation

A decision-relevant question tied to a buying stage, stakeholder, observation condition, and commercial owner.

Buyer Conversation Map

The Sprint artifact prioritizing 20–40 conversations across category inclusion, comparison, evaluation, objections, procurement, and validation.

Observation condition

The documented date, environment, question, market, account state, language, and other context needed to interpret an observation.

Source gap

A missing, weak, conflicting, inaccessible, or unsupported source condition relevant to a buyer question.

Shortlist / Source Gap Map

The Sprint artifact connecting buyer conversations to observed source patterns, alternatives, confidence gaps, and the minimum defensible action.

Proof surface

An owned or approved place where a buyer can inspect evidence about fit, implementation, security, integration, outcome, or another material claim.

Proof & Conversion Review

The Sprint review of whether an AI-referred or otherwise informed buyer can validate material claims and move through the relevant owned path.

90-Day Action Register

The Sprint artifact listing five to eight priority actions with named owners, dependencies, acceptance tests, and measures.

Evidence vs inference

ARM labels what a source directly supports separately from the interpretation or recommendation derived from it.

Buyer-validation path

The sequence of proof, product, implementation, security, service, and conversion surfaces a buyer may use to resolve a decision.
