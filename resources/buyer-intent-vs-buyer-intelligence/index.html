Intent vs intelligence

A surge is a signal. It is not a sales strategy.

Buyer intent can indicate that research may be happening. Buyer intelligence asks what decision is forming, which stakeholders matter, what evidence they need, and which action your team can responsibly take.

Signal ≠ intent certaintyAccount ≠ buying groupScore ≠ explanationAction needs an owner

The category error behind most intent programs

Activity is often promoted into certainty: a topic surge becomes purchase intent, an account score becomes urgency, and urgency becomes an automated task. Each step adds inference. When those assumptions are invisible, reps inherit noise and buyers receive outreach that ignores their actual decision.

When an intent signal becomes useful

01

It has provenance

The team knows the source, coverage, freshness, and permitted use.

02

It maps to a buyer question

The signal connects to a real evaluation, comparison, implementation, or proof concern.

03

It respects the buying group

The action accounts for champions, economic buyers, evaluators, blockers, and missing roles.

04

It produces a bounded action

A named owner receives an approved response with timing, evidence, and a stop condition.

Do not collapse uncertainty

A high-intent threshold can prioritize review. It does not prove that a particular person is buying, that a deal will close, or that automated outreach is appropriate.

Use both—but give them different jobs

Intent system

Detect change

Monitor selected account, topic, usage, or engagement conditions and preserve their limits.

Intelligence system

Make the change legible

Connect the signal to the buying group, decision question, supporting evidence, and owner-led response.

The goal is not more alerts.

The goal is a revenue team that can explain why a signal matters, who should act, what evidence supports the action, and how the result will be reviewed.
