GEO vs SEO

GEO vs SEO: What Changed, What Didn't, and What to Do About It

SEO got you onto the search results page. GEO gets you into the answer. They're not the same discipline — but they're not competitors either. Here's the full landscape.

The confusion between GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) and SEO (Search Engine Optimization) is the most common question ARM Agency gets from enterprise teams. The short answer: SEO is the foundation, GEO is the structure built on top of it. You need both. But understanding what changed — and what didn't — determines where to invest.

The core difference: ranking vs generation

SEO optimizes for position on a search results page. The model is simple: rank higher for a keyword, get more clicks, convert more visitors. The search engine returns links, and the user decides which to click.

GEO optimizes for presence in a synthesized answer. The generative engine doesn't return links — it constructs an answer. The user may never click through to your site. The answer is the destination. Your goal shifts from "rank for this keyword" to "be recognized as an entity the model includes in its synthesis."

The reframeSEO: "Can the search engine find my page?" → GEO: "Does the generative engine know who I am and what to say about me?"

What SEO still does well (and why you shouldn't abandon it)

Generative engines still retrieve web content to ground their answers. If your pages aren't crawled, indexed, and findable, the model has nothing to retrieve. SEO is the input layer — without it, GEO has nothing to work with.

Additionally, traditional search hasn't gone away. Google still processes billions of queries per day, and SEO still drives organic traffic. The two disciplines serve different engines with different paradigms, and most companies need to invest in both.

What GEO does that SEO can't

SEO can't tell a language model who you are. It can't map your entity to authority nodes. It can't ensure the model synthesizes an accurate answer when someone asks about you. These are the things GEO does:

  • Entity clarity: Structured data that tells the model who you are — not just what your page says
  • Evidence context: Keeping material claims connected to source records a reader can inspect
  • Observation baseline: Documenting relevant pages, sources, access conditions, and current public observations before a decision
  • Technical access: Reviewing whether public evidence is available through the relevant crawl and page paths

Does SEO still matter with AI search?

Yes — and this is the point most "SEO is dead" articles get wrong. SEO isn't dead; it's insufficient. A generative engine that retrieves web content to ground its answer needs to find your content. If your SEO is broken, the model can't access you — and no amount of entity schema will help if the underlying content isn't crawlable.

The relationship is sequential: SEO makes your content findable, GEO makes your entity recognizable. Skipping SEO to go straight to GEO is like building a roof without a foundation. Skipping GEO because your SEO is strong is like building a foundation and never putting a roof on it.

AEO vs GEO vs SEO: the full landscape

There are three related disciplines, and they're often confused:

DisciplineWhat it optimizesEngine type
SEOKeyword rankingsSearch engines (Google, Bing)
AEOFeatured snippets, answer boxesSearch engines with extractive answers
GEOEntity recognition, citation, synthesisGenerative engines (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity)

AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) sits between SEO and GEO. It optimizes content to appear in featured snippets and answer boxes on traditional search engines. GEO goes further — it ensures the model recognizes you as an entity and includes you in its synthesized answer, not just an extractive snippet.

FAQ

Is GEO replacing SEO?
No. GEO is the layer above SEO, not a replacement. SEO ensures your content is findable. GEO ensures your entity is recognizable. You need both — SEO is the foundation, GEO is the structure built on top.
What's the difference between GEO and AEO?
AEO optimizes content for featured snippets and answer boxes on search engines. GEO optimizes for being recognized and cited inside synthesized answers from generative AI models. AEO is about extractive answers; GEO is about generative synthesis.
Does SEO still matter if I'm doing GEO?
Yes. Generative engines still retrieve web content. Strong SEO ensures your pages are crawled and available. Without SEO, there's nothing for GEO to structure. The two disciplines are sequential, not competitive.
How should a team evaluate GEO work?
Start with a documented decision context, evidence, technical-access conditions, and an observation baseline. The accepted scope should define any later review without promising a third-party answer, citation, or visibility outcome.
Can a team begin GEO work itself?
Many technical and editorial fundamentals can be completed in-house. A fit review can help decide whether the available evidence, access conditions, and decision context warrant a fixed-scope external review.
